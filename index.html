How the forecast works

  1. Four methods each forecast the record, using different views of its trend and seasonal cycle.
  2. Every 3 months over the past 12 years, later data is hidden and each method forecasts up to 4 years ahead. The forecast is then compared with what happened.
  3. Methods with smaller past errors get more weight in the blend. During testing, the weights use only errors known at that date.
  4. The shaded band comes from past blend errors, and it should contain about 80% of future values.

October 2026 and October 2028

"Change" compares the 12 months to October 2026 with the 12 months to October 2028, so the seasonal cycle does not affect it. This is why the change can differ from the gap between the two single months. A change is called clear when it is larger than the usual error of that comparison in past tests.

Data sources

    Records are published with a delay, so months without data are estimated and marked. Forecasts are statistical. They do not simulate the climate, and events such as a strong El Niño or a large eruption can move values outside the band.
